## Humane Exclusion & Post-Exclusion Cleanup

Bats are often found in older buildings due to their ability to squeeze through surprisingly small openings – typically at roof vents, chimneys, and soffits. While initial exclusion is crucial, a successful bat removal project demands a comprehensive approach focusing on long-term prevention and thorough cleanup. This article details the steps involved in a humane one-way exclusion process, sealing strategies, and the critical importance of guano removal and histoplasmosis prevention.

### One-Way Exclusion Devices

One-way exclusion devices, such as electronic bat boxes, allow bats to exit but prevent re-entry. These devices are typically installed over existing entry points. KC Pest Experts uses proven methods to ensure a high rate of successful exclusion, minimizing bat stress and maximizing the effectiveness of the process.  It’s vital to understand that simply installing a device isn’t enough; bats may return if the primary entry point isn’t addressed. 

### Sealing Entry Points

Once bats have exited, it’s essential to seal all potential entry points. This involves using durable, bat-resistant materials like expanding foam, steel wool, and sealant.  Careful attention must be paid to gaps around flashing, vents, and chimneys.  Consider consulting with a professional to assess the long-term integrity of your sealing strategy. 

### Guano Removal & Histoplasmosis Prevention

Bat guano is a significant concern due to the potential for histoplasmosis, a respiratory infection caused by spores in the droppings. *Always* wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE), including a respirator, gloves, and eye protection, when handling guano. Thorough cleaning with a disinfectant solution is essential. Proper cleanup minimizes health risks and creates a safer environment.  [USDA’s Guide to Bat Guano](https://www.usda.gov/visit/wildlife-disease/bat-guano-and-histoplasmosis) offers valuable information on managing this risk.
